# 5. THE DAMIEN CENTER *(care and support)*

**Headline:** Nobody should face a hard diagnosis alone.
**Intro:** The Troy Damien Center is a hospitality center for neighbors living with serious illness. What started in the 1980s as a refuge for people affected by HIV/AIDS — at a time when few doors were open to them — is today a warm community for anyone navigating serious illness: a place for meals, friendship, peer support, and help finding the services you need.

### What you'll find here
- A hot lunch and a seat at the table, weekdays ⚑ *(ties to food schedule — verify)*
- Social events, games, and recreation — because joy is part of health
- Peer support from people who've been where you are
- Help connecting to medical, housing, and benefit services

**Our history matters to us.** The Damien Center's roots in the HIV/AIDS crisis taught us everything about welcome without judgment. That welcome now extends to every neighbor facing illness.

**Contact:** Barbara Healey, Damien Center Coordinator — (518) 274-5920 x206

# 10. ABOUT

### Our story
**Headline:** Forty years of showing up.
In 1986, congregations across the Troy area decided their neighborhood needed them to work as one. Led by Rev. Seymour Flinn, Rev. Don Pangburn, and other founders, they consolidated their ministries into one organization with a simple charge: *work for a more abundant life for the people of the area.*

Four decades later, that work looks like lunch on the table, a bed delivered before dark, a teen's first paycheck, and a building on Second Street with its doors open. We were founded by people of faith, and we welcome people of every faith and no faith at all — the only requirement for belonging here is being a neighbor.

**Milestones:**
- **1986** — Founded through the consolidation of Troy-area congregation ministries
- **1997** — First MLK Scholarship awarded
- **2004** — Bought and renovated our home at 392 Second Street
- **2011** — 25th anniversary capital campaign
- **2016** — 30th anniversary; Legacy Fund established
- **2024** — Building renovations completed through a Lowe's Hometown Grant; mural painted on our Second Street wall ⚑ *(mural date/artist unknown — get artist name for credit!)*
- **2026** — Forty years, forty flames
